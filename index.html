What this does not model

Embodied carbon

Manufacturing, shipping, installation and disposal of appliances, batteries, wiring or the device running this page are outside the boundary.

Rebound effects

A lower-carbon schedule can make a load feel cheaper or cleaner and encourage more use. The model keeps energy demand fixed and does not predict behavior change.

Tariffs and bills

No time-of-use tariff, demand charge, tax, fixed fee, solar export rule or utility bill is modeled. Carbon-optimal hours are not automatically bill-optimal hours.

Efficiency losses

Standby draw, inverter loss, power factor, motor ramps, thermostat cycles, heating inefficiency and partial-load behavior are simplified into constant nameplate watts.

Measured marginal emissions

The three curves are synthetic average-intensity shapes. They are not locational marginal emissions, real-time grid telemetry or a forecast for any named balancing authority.

Household infrastructure

Panel capacity, wire sizing, breaker coordination, voltage drop, solar self-consumption, battery round trips and utility demand-response rules are not simulated.

Model contract

PeakShift divides the day into 24 hourly carbon-intensity slots. Each appliance run is rounded up to whole-hour circuit reservations, while emissions are prorated by the actual run minutes inside those hours. One-shot appliances must use consecutive hours. Repeatable appliances may split across non-consecutive hours. Deadlines are hard at the preferred hour plus the declared flexibility.

The solver enumerates legal hour placements and uses exact branch-and-bound search per circuit. It never double-books an hour on one circuit, uses deterministic cost and lexicographic tie-breaking, and reports an honest search-space error instead of silently approximating when an instance is too large.
